Space payloads rely on stable electrical, thermal, and mechanical performance throughout launch, vacuum exposure, thermal cycling, and mission operation. For payload enclosure designers, electromagnetic interference shielding is a critical part of protecting RF payloads, avionics modules, and payload data handling units from unwanted emissions and susceptibility risks.

Parker Chomerics CHO-SEAL1285 and CHO-SEAL 1215 conductive elastomer gaskets are commonly evaluated for EMI shielding applications where environmental sealing, compression behavior, material compatibility, and low-outgassing requirements must be considered together. Final material selection should always be based on current Parker documentation, project-specific requirements, and applicable test data, including NASA/ASTM E595-style outgassing requirements where relevant.

 

CHO-SEAL 1285 for Payload EMI Shielding

CHO-SEAL 1285 is a conductive elastomer gasket material used in EMI shielding applications where enclosure seams, access panels, covers, and module interfaces require electrical continuity.

 

For space payload enclosure evaluation, CHO-SEAL 1285 may be considered for:

  • RF payload module housings
  • Payload electronics box covers
  • Instrument control enclosures
  • Connector panel interfaces
  • Shielded access panels and inspection covers
  • Payload data handling unit seams

 

CHO-SEAL 1215 for Payload EMI Shielding

CHO-SEAL 1215 is also used in conductive elastomer EMI gasket applications where shielding continuity and mechanical sealing must be considered together.

 

For payload enclosure designs, CHO-SEAL 1215 may be evaluated for:

  • Avionics payload module seams
  • Sensor electronics compartments
  • RF and mixed-signal electronics covers
  • Payload processing and storage assemblies
  • Removable shielded covers
  • Conductive enclosure interfaces

Key evaluation points include:

  • EMI frequency range of concern
  • Required closure force
  • Compression set behavior
  • Mating surface material and finish
  • Thermal cycling profile
  • Environmental sealing needs
  • Contamination-control requirements
  • Available gasket form, profile, or fabrication option

 

Why Low Outgassing Matters for Space Payloads

Low outgassing is especially important for payloads containing optical surfaces, detectors, sensors, filters, thermal control coatings, or contamination-sensitive electronics. In vacuum, volatile materials can migrate from polymers and elastomers. These species may then condense on colder or sensitive surfaces.

 

Potential impacts include:

  • Reduced optical transmission
  • Detector performance degradation
  • Contamination of lenses, mirrors, or filters
  • Changes to thermal control surface properties
  • Increased risk to precision measurement instruments
  • Material review delays during payload qualification

Key Design Considerations

Conductive elastomer gasket performance depends on both material properties and enclosure design. A high-performing gasket cannot compensate for poor flange design, insufficient compression, or incompatible surface finishes.

When evaluating CHO-SEAL 1285 or CHO-SEAL 1215, consider:

  • EMI shielding performance
    Confirm the gasket supports the required enclosure-level shielding performance across the relevant frequency range.
  • Compression control
    Design the flange, groove, and fastener pattern to maintain the recommended gasket compression without over-compressing the material.
  • Fastener spacing and flange rigidity
    Prevent flange bowing and localized under-compression that can create EMI leakage paths.
  • Surface conductivity
    Ensure mating surfaces support low-resistance electrical contact and are compatible with the selected gasket.
  • Galvanic compatibility
    Review the gasket filler system, enclosure alloy, plating, conversion coating, and expected ground-handling environment.
  • Thermal cycling
    Evaluate compression retention, contact resistance, and mechanical stability after mission-relevant temperature exposure.
  • Vacuum compatibility
    Review vacuum exposure requirements and applicable material test data.
  • Contamination control
    Confirm outgassing and handling requirements for payloads with optical, detector, sensor, or contamination-sensitive assemblies.
  • Service access
    Consider whether the cover or panel will be opened during integration, test, or maintenance, and evaluate gasket durability accordingly.
