At Parker Chomerics, there are several different types of electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding particles that are used in our materials. The two most commonly used particles, especially for high-performance applications, are silver-plated aluminum particles and nickel-plated aluminum particles. To ensure the right product is selected for an application, it’s important to understand the unique benefits and limitations of each particle.

First, let’s explore silver-plated aluminum particles, which are created when silver plating is placed over an aluminum core. Because silver is inherently very conductive, it provides very high conductivity of the gasket or the coating or paint itself as well as very high levels of EMI shielding. Additionally, it offers a high level of corrosion resistance against common substrates including most aluminum and coated or plated substrates.

On the other side, you have nickel-plated particles. This is a newer particle that has been in use for about 10-15 years. While it hasn’t been utilized as long as silver-plated aluminum, it does offer many exciting benefits that make it an excellent fit for some applications. Nickel-plated aluminum particles offer very high levels of shielding effectiveness across most of the frequence spectrum, even higher so than most silver-plated aluminum. Additionally, it provides the highest level of corrosion resistance against aluminum substrates and even against nickel-plated substrates. However, one caveat to be aware of is that nickel itself is not highly conductive. That means as a grounding gasket it does not have the same level of conductivity as silver-plated aluminum gaskets do.

Despite this potential limitation, nickel-plated particles are becoming more and more common due to the cost of silver. Silver is far more expensive than nickel, and there is a fairly high level of volatility in silver commodity pricing. Nickel-plated aluminum gasketing or nickel-plated particles within EMI shielding solutions present a potential alternative that is more cost-effective.

 

Silver-plated aluminum continues to be a popular choice for certain applications. For example, it is approved for use in many defense and aerospace applications and meets military specifications. However, nickel-plated aluminum can be a more cost-effective solution across other applications, especially ones that are going to see corrosion and harsh environments.

At the end of the day, the primary drawback with nickel is the lower conductivity, but this downside is often outweighed by the lower cost as well as higher levels of corrosion resistance and EMI shielding. To determine which material may be the right choice, it really just depends on the specific application.
