From dental procedures to aesthetic techniques and robotic operations, innovations in medical device applications are increasing treatment options and improving patient outcomes in many areas. Examples of this technology include diagnostic equipment, patient monitoring equipment, aesthetic laser systems and electronic systems that are used in outpatient procedures. 

While medical devices vary in size and intended use, one thing they all have in common is that they contain some kind of an electronic system with many controls. And in general, most of them require electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding as well as electrical grounding. 

That’s where many products from Parker Chomerics such as the SOFT-SHIELD® product family and other conductive coatings come into play. Circuit boards contain enclosures that need to be grounded and shielded. In these situations, a conductive foam ends up being a very highly sought-after commercial product because it has effective shielding, a relatively low cost and design flexibility. Conductive foams can be provided in fabric-over-foam strips that can be cut down to size, and they can also be die cut and used as a perimeter gasket around specific enclosures. 

 

There are additional solutions within conductive foams that can be used such as silicone foams, as opposed to urethane foams. Silicone foam is a much better alternative to urethane foams. For example, our SOFT-SHIELD 3700 and SOFT-SHIELD 3800 are silicone-based or silicone foam-based materials that have a higher operating temperature range. This is important for circuit boards, which are electronics that need a slightly higher temperature.

Many of these housings are made of plastics or sometimes a coated metal that requires an additional EMI shielding or electrically conductive coating. Plastics provide lightweighting, environmental durability and protection from the elements. In these cases, product designers can use EMI shielding or a conductive coating applied on the inside in order to provide that EMI shielding while still maintaining lightweight. Some conductive coatings are silver-based. A key benefit of silver is that it's inherently antimicrobial which can be very beneficial in medical device applications.

It’s important to note that most of these medical device applications are well-controlled environmentally, which means there is not much risk in terms of exposure to elements. Most of the time they're indoors in a room temperature environment, reducing concerns when it comes to the temperature of the board itself.

To determine the right EMI shielding solution, it's important to look at the combination of performance and commercial considerations. There are significant regulatory requirements from an FDA perspective as well as global requirements when it comes to EMI shielding. Different countries have different standards, so oftentimes devices need to go through either more or less rigorous testing depending on where that company is trying to expand and build out a market.
