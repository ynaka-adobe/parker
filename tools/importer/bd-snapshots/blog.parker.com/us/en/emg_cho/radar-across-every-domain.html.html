Modern radar architectures—whether ground-based air surveillance, shipboard multifunction arrays, or airborne AESA systems—depend on consistent radio frequency (RF) integrity, environmental sealing, and pressure equalization across multiple line-replaceable units (LRUs). Parker Chomerics electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding gaskets and conductive vents are commonly applied at enclosure seams, access panels, connector interfaces, and pressure-equalization points to help designers balance shielding effectiveness, sealing, durability, and maintenance across harsh operating domains.

Each domain presents a unique set of electromagnetic and environmental challenges that can compromise the mission’s success if the wrong material is selected or the material is omitted altogether. 

 

First, let’s examine the key selection criteria for determining appropriate shielding materials.

EMI Shielding and Electrical Interfaces

  • Contact integrity under vibration and shock: stable metal-to-metal conduction across seams
  • Bonding across coated surfaces: performance depends on mating finishes and compression
  • Broadband behavior: seams/vents behave differently than solid walls, and continuity at joints matters

Environmental Sealing and Survivability

  • Water and humidity sealing at removable access points
  • Dust/sand exclusion for ground systems and expeditionary shelters
  • Salt fog exposure and galvanic compatibility for shipboard and coastal installations

Mechanical Durability

  • Compression set/stress relaxation: long-life sealing requires stable recovery over time as well as temperature cycling
  • Cycle life: repeated open/close maintenance without losing EMI contact or environmental seal
  • Vibration: materials must maintain contact pressure and resist fretting at interfaces

Airflow and Pressure Equalization

  • Venting needs: cooling airflow, fan plenums, and pressure equalization without creating EMI leakage
  • Altitude/pressure cycling: airborne and some naval topside enclosures experience significant differential pressures

Manufacturing, Installation, and Repair

  • Tolerance management: flange flatness, gap variability, and compression control
  • Field maintainability: replaceability, damage tolerance, and rework

Ground Radar (Fixed, Mobile, and Expeditionary Systems)

Ground-based radars often see dust/sand, wide temperature swings, and repeated service events. EMI control is frequently challenged by large enclosures, door seams, and numerous cable/connector interfaces, and the recommended approach to EMI shielding will vary by interface.

For large access doors, perimeter seams, and frequent maintenance panels, fingerstock gaskets are an excellent option for high-cycle access where repeated opening/closing is expected. It should be used where the primary need is reliable metal-to-metal EMI contact across a seam with mechanical tolerance variation.

For LRU covers and moderate-gap seams needing both EMI shielding and sealing, conductive elastomer gaskets such as CHO-SEAL® 6503 or CHO-SEAL 1298 can provide EMI shielding plus environmental sealing in one interface. Conductive elastomers help maintain a controlled seal line around electronics covers and connector panels. These materials should be selected based on mating finish, galvanic compatibility, and expected compression range.

For complex geometry, retrofit sealing, or tolerance-driven seams, CHO-FORM® 5560 form-in-place gasketing helps address irregular flanges, variable gaps, and intricate perimeters without custom die-cut parts. This gasketing is useful for prototyping and mixed-hardware builds.

Finally, for cooling openings and pressure equalization without compromising shielding, CHO-CELL™ vent panels and SHIELD-CELL™ vent panels provide airflow/pressure equalization while reducing EMI leakage risk at ventilation points. Use SHIELD-CELL where vent openings are large or where maximum shielding margin is required, and use CHO-CELL where compact venting is needed at the LRU level.

Solutions for Specific Radar Scenarios

The environment where each radar will be utilized will determine which EMI shielding and sealing solutions are most appropriate. Here are three commonly encountered scenarios.

1. Ground Radar: The Battle Against Dust and Durability

Ground-based systems, such as mobile missile defense units or coastal surveillance towers, are exposed to extreme temperature swings, UV radiation, and abrasive debris. Ground radar often features large access panels and ruggedized enclosures that must withstand physical impact and high-vibration environments. For these applications, steel vent panels are the gold standard. Unlike lightweight aluminum, steel vents provide the physical durability to resist field damage while maintaining the waveguides-beyond-cutoff properties needed for high-frequency shielding. When paired with fingerstock gaskets for high-traffic access doors, you get a system that maintains shielding integrity even after hundreds of maintenance cycles.

2. Air Radar: The Weight-to-Performance Ratio

In the aerospace world, every gram matters. Airborne radars, like the AESA arrays in modern fighters, must be exceptionally lightweight without sacrificing shielding effectiveness across wide-frequency bands. High altitudes bring rapid thermal cycling and the risk of outgassing in vacuum-like conditions. CHO-SEAL 6503 (nickel-plated graphite-filled fluorosilicone) is specifically engineered for this challenge. Its fluorosilicone binder resists the aviation fuels and cleaning chemicals common on flight lines, while the nickel-graphite filler provides excellent shielding without the weight penalty of pure silver. For internal partitions where space is at a premium, CHO-FORM 5560 allows us to dispense a precision EMI bead directly onto the housing, saving weight and assembly time.

3. Sea Radar: The Fight Against Galvanic Corrosion

The maritime environment is perhaps the most hostile for electronics. Salt fog acts as an electrolyte, turning every EMI seal into a potential battery that accelerates corrosion. "Dissimilar metal corrosion" occurs when silver-filled gaskets touch aluminum housings in the presence of salt spray. This can destroy the electrical bond, causing the radar to "go blind" to certain frequencies. This is where CHO-SEAL 1298 excels. Utilizing silver-plated aluminum particles allows us to match the galvanic potential of the aluminum housing more closely, significantly slowing the corrosion process. To protect air intakes, SHIELD-CELL vents with specialized plating offer the high-volume airflow needed for cooling while providing a robust barrier against the corrosive sea air.
