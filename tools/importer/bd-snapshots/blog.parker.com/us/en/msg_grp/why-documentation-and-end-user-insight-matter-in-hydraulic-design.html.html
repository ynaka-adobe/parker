Hydraulic system challenges are rarely caused by one obvious issue. More often, they trace back to small details such as an undocumented circuit, an overlooked subsystem interaction or a design assumption that does not reflect how a machine is used in the field. Parker engineers support customers by helping them think through the details that can shape hydraulic system performance, reliability and manufacturability.

Brad Nicol, senior project engineer at Hydraulic Pump and Motor Division has built his career around helping teams work through those details with a practical, methodical approach.

He has spent 25 years working with hydraulic pumps, motors and systems. Today, he specializes in Parker’s hydrostatic pumps & motors and manages an R&D lab, where Parker supports product development, testing and qualification.

His perspective is grounded in practical problem-solving, disciplined troubleshooting and a deep respect for the people who operate and maintain machines every day.

“It is satisfying to break a complex challenge down into smaller pieces to get at the core of how things work. I’m constantly amazed at how the smallest details can make a major impact.”

 

Brad Nicol, senior project engineer, Parker Hannifin

Nicol grew up on a family farm, where solving real-world problems was part of everyday life. That early exposure helped shape the way he approaches engineering today.

Engineering became a way for him to better understand how the world works and contribute solutions that matter in the field. Over the course of his Parker career, Nicol has worked on many machine types, with his most extensive experience in applications where reliability and performance are critical like mining and large material handling equipment.

Nicol often supports Parker application engineers and account managers when specific issues or opportunities require deeper product knowledge or technical support.

That connection between field insight and engineering expertise is central to how Parker helps customers address complex integration, performance optimization, cost pressures and tight deadlines.

Avoiding engineering problems before they become costly

For Nicol, the true value of engineering lies not just in solving problems, but in helping teams anticipate and avoid them.

In hydraulic system design, that often starts with clarity. Poor documentation practices can create confusion as projects develop, especially when multiple teams are making decisions across design, testing, procurement and manufacturing.

For engineers managing specification requirements and cost targets, Nicol emphasizes the importance of addressing small details early. A well-documented system can reduce confusion, support faster troubleshooting and improve confidence as a design moves toward qualification.

For example, Nicol notes that it is difficult to troubleshoot a hydraulic system without a circuit. Even simple systems should be written down. Clear circuit drawings are valuable because they help engineering, service and support work from the same understanding.

Another common issue Nicol sees is teams may focus heavily on major components while overlooking seemingly insignificant details that can cause larger problems later.

“Stay calm and methodical. Isolate components or subsystems so efforts can be focused, and step back to reevaluate what has been learned.”

 

Brad Nicol, senior project engineer, Parker Hannifin

For customers managing tight development schedules or complex machine platforms, that kind of discipline can help reduce rework, improve communication and support better decisions under pressure.

The engineering details behind one of Parker’s most popular hydraulic series

Nicol’s work includes supporting Parker GOLD CUP® pumps and motors, a series built for severe-duty applications where pressure, speed, durability and uptime are critical.

The product line is used across demanding markets such as mining, oil and gas, marine, energy, industrial, rubber and tire processing, lumber and wood and construction.

The GOLD CUP® series is engineered to support longer life and increased uptime. Its robust barrel-bearing design, hydraulically controlled rocker cam and reliable pressure control help reduce stress on the hydraulic system. 

Fast pressure compensation helps protect the pump and surrounding components during pressure peaks, reducing system shock and supporting longer component life.

For demanding applications such as mining, drilling, marine, construction, material handling, logging, shredding, cranes and winches, those advantages can translate into lower operating costs, improved productivity and reduced downtime. 

For Nicol, reliable performance often comes down to the details. That same principle is reflected in the GOLD CUP® series, where design features help reduce operating costs, manage system shock, extend component life and improve uptime.

Advice for Machine Designers

When asked what advice he would give machine designers, Nicol points first to collaboration.

“No one can be an expert at everything. Find a strong team, engage others throughout the design process and get as close to the end user as possible.”

 

Brad Nicol, senior project engineer, Parker Hannifin

Operators and maintenance personnel often have a lot to teach if engineers listen with an open mind. When possible, Nicol encourages designers to experience their challenges firsthand.

This approach reflects the broader value Parker delivers to customers. Its experienced engineers collaborate across teams, support complex projects and help solve real application challenges.
