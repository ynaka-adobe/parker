For OEMs demanding industrial applications, hydraulic cylinders often need to work at the edge of size, pressure, stroke length, certification requirements and real-world operating uncertainty.

The engineering challenge is not only to deliver reliable motion and control performance, but to anticipate complex load cases, communicate design intent clearly and solve problems collaboratively when applications push beyond standard solutions.

Parker helps address these challenges with decades of hydraulic actuation expertise, close customer engagement and hands-on engineering practices that review how components will truly perform from the shop floor to the field.

Miles Gilbert-Morgan, a principal engineer at the Hydraulic Actuator Division, is one example of the many Parker engineers globally who apply their hydraulic actuation expertise and practical engineering tools to help customers develop custom cylinder solutions.

“Engineering is a creative and collaborative process. Our customers are always coming up with new applications that keep me learning.”

 

Miles Gilbert-Morgan, principal engineer, Parker Hannifin

Custom Hydraulic Cylinders for Demanding Industrial Applications

OEMs and equipment builders in oil & gas, lumber production, marine steering and other heavy-duty industries often face motion and control challenges that cannot be solved with standard cylinder designs. Applications may involve extreme loads, long strokes, high pressures, harsh environments, certification requirements and limited opportunities for prototype testing.

The challenge is not only whether a cylinder meets the initial specification but whether it will perform reliably when the machine is placed into service and exposed to the full range of operating conditions.

This is when Parker’s hydraulic actuator expertise becomes invaluable.

One early example from Gilbert-Morgan’s career illustrates the complexity of such a hydraulic cylinder application.

A new customer required several large, custom telescopic cylinders for a prototype oil rig. The cylinders were designed, built and shipped in just sixteen weeks.

However, once in the field, the customer reported that the cylinders would not retract at high flow rates and, in some conditions, would begin to extend.

The issue was traced to a second load case that had not been fully anticipated during the initial design process.

Gilbert-Morgan analyzed valve flow response data and helped present a corrective plan which resolved the issue. The customer has continued ordering the cylinder for more than two decades.

Now with 26 years of experience in custom hydraulic cylinder design, Gilbert-Morgan brings deep technical expertise and a collaborative approach to helping customers solve application-specific challenges in demanding motion and control environments.

 “In the custom cylinder world where the prototype is the product, and oftentimes boundaries are being pushed or new uses explored, not everything is black and white.” 

Taking a Practical Approach to Engineering Challenges

For Gilbert-Morgan, effective engineering depends on communication, observation and disciplined problem solving. 

“Engineering is much more about communication than many people realize.”

This emphasis on communication can be seen in his drawings, which he views as a primary way to communicate design intent. Clear, consistent drawing layouts help reduce misunderstanding and support smoother execution.

When troubleshooting difficult applications, Gilbert-Morgan combines practical observation with structured tools. He values Gemba, uses Fishbone diagrams and learns from colleagues, trusted vendors or prior work.

His advice to machine designers is start with the fundamentals, understand load paths, test assumptions with estimates and boundaries, seek mentors and keep learning.

Future Trends in Industrial Hydraulics

That perspective is important as the broader motion and control industry continues to change. Electrification, smarter system architectures, advanced simulation tools and new manufacturing technologies are presenting new possibilities for hydraulic systems.

“The combination of electrification with hydraulics on mobile applications will be transformative in many ways and drive efficiency gains that will spread to industrial applications as well.”
